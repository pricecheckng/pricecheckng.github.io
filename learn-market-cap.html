What is market cap in crypto?

A free beginner lesson. Learn why price alone can fool you, and how to compare coins properly.

1. The simple formula

Market cap = price of one coin × number of coins in circulation. It tells you how much the whole coin is worth, not how much one coin costs.

2. Why price alone is misleading

A coin at $0.01 is not "cheap" and a coin at $50,000 is not "expensive". What matters is how many coins exist. A coin that costs $0.01 but has 100 billion coins in circulation is worth $1 billion in total. A coin that costs $100 with only one million coins is worth $100 million, which is ten times smaller.

So before you think "this coin can easily reach $1", ask: what would the market cap be at that price? Multiply $1 by the number of coins. If the answer is bigger than most of the crypto market, it is very unlikely.

3. Rank

Coins are ranked by market cap. Rank #1 has the biggest market cap. Higher-ranked coins are usually older, trade more and are easier to buy and sell. Lower-ranked coins can move much more, up and down, and are harder to sell in a hurry.

4. Market cap versus volume

  • Market cap: the total value of all coins.
  • 24h volume: how much was traded in the last 24 hours.

High volume compared with market cap means a lot of activity. Very low volume means few people are trading, so it can be hard to sell at a fair price.

5. What market cap does not tell you

  • It does not mean the money is "inside" the coin. Nobody put that much money in. It is price times supply.
  • It does not show if the coin is safe or a good buy.
  • Some coins also have coins not yet released. Check how many coins will exist in total.
