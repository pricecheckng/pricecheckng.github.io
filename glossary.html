Crypto glossary

Plain-English meanings of the words you see on PriceCheck NG. Not financial advice.

All-time high (ATH)
The highest price a coin has ever reached. Coin pages on PriceCheck NG show how far today's price is below that peak.
Altcoin
Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin. Many altcoins are smaller and riskier, and they often move more than Bitcoin in both directions.
Bear market
A long period when prices keep falling and people feel negative about the market. There is no fixed rule, but a fall of 20% or more from a recent high is a common way to describe it.
Bitcoin dominance
Bitcoin's share of the total crypto market cap, shown as a percent. When dominance rises, money is often moving from altcoins into Bitcoin, but it is only a rough signal. See the market chart.
Blockchain
A shared record of transactions kept by many computers at once. Records that are already written are very hard to change, which is why people trust it without a bank in the middle.
Bull market
A long period when prices keep rising and people feel positive about the market.
Candlestick
A bar on a price chart that shows the open, high, low and close for one period of time. Green usually means the price rose in that period and red means it fell. Learn more in How to read a price chart.
Centralised exchange (CEX)
A company that runs a crypto exchange and holds your coins for you, such as Binance or Bybit. It is easy to use, but you depend on the company, and it may ask for ID.
Circulating supply
The number of coins that are out in the market today and can be traded. PriceCheck NG multiplies it by the price to get the market cap.
Coin and token
A coin runs on its own blockchain, like Bitcoin or Ether. A token is built on top of another blockchain, like USDT on Ethereum or Tron.
Cold wallet
A wallet that keeps your keys offline, usually on a small hardware device. It is safer from online attacks, but if you lose the device and the backup words, the coins are gone.
Custodial wallet
A wallet where a company, usually an exchange, holds the keys for you. If that company fails or freezes your account, you can lose access to your coins.
Decentralised exchange (DEX)
An exchange that runs on a blockchain through smart contracts. You trade from your own wallet and no company holds your coins. See the DEX category.
DeFi (decentralised finance)
Financial services such as lending, borrowing and trading that run on blockchains through code instead of banks. Bugs and scams are real risks, so many people start small. See all categories.
Fear & Greed Index
A score from 0 to 100 that shows the mood of the crypto market. A low score means fear and a high score means greed. Read the plain guide or see today's index.
Fully diluted value (FDV)
The price multiplied by the maximum supply, or by the total supply when there is no maximum. It shows what the coin would be worth if every coin already existed. It can be much bigger than the market cap.
Gas fee
The fee you pay to send a transaction on a blockchain such as Ethereum. It goes up when the network is busy. See today's Ethereum gas fees.
Halving
An event about every four years when the reward for mining new Bitcoin blocks is cut in half. It slows the rate at which new Bitcoin is created. See the Bitcoin network page.
Honeypot
A scam token that you can buy but cannot sell. The code blocks sales, so your money is trapped. See how to avoid scams.
Layer 1 and Layer 2
A Layer 1 is a main blockchain, such as Bitcoin, Ethereum or Solana. A Layer 2 is a network built on top of one to make transactions cheaper and faster, such as Arbitrum or Base.
Liquidity
How easily you can buy or sell a coin without moving its price much. Coins with low liquidity can jump or drop sharply on small trades.
Market cap
The price of a coin multiplied by its circulating supply. It shows how big a coin is, not how much money is inside it. Learn more in What market cap really means.
Maximum supply
The most coins that can ever exist. Bitcoin's maximum supply is 21 million. Many coins have no maximum.
Memecoin
A coin built around a joke or internet culture, such as Dogecoin, Shiba Inu or Pepe. Prices can move enormously in a day, and many have little real use. See the meme category.
Mempool
The waiting area for transactions that have been sent but not yet confirmed. A full mempool means slower confirmations and higher fees. See the Bitcoin network page.
NFT
A unique digital item recorded on a blockchain, such as artwork or a game item. Unlike a coin, one NFT is not interchangeable with another. See the NFT category.
P2P trading
Trading directly with another person on an exchange's marketplace, often paying by bank transfer. In Nigeria it is a common way to buy and sell USDT for naira. Check every offer and follow the platform's rules. See the USDT to naira P2P rate.
Pump and dump
A scheme where a group pushes a price up with hype, then sells to the people who bought late. The price then collapses. See how to avoid scams.
Rug pull
A scam where the creators of a project take the investors' money and disappear, often by draining the trading pool.
Seed phrase
A list of 12 or 24 words that can restore a crypto wallet. Anyone who has it controls your coins, so never share it or type it into a website.
Slippage
The difference between the price you expected and the price you actually got. It is larger when liquidity is low or the price is moving fast.
Spread
The gap between the best price a buyer is offering and the best price a seller is asking. A small spread usually means a busy market.
Stablecoin
A coin designed to stay at a steady value, usually one US dollar, like USDT or USDC. It is not risk-free, because a stablecoin can lose its peg. See stablecoin health and the plain guide.
Staking
Locking up coins to help run a proof-of-stake blockchain and earn rewards. Rewards are not guaranteed, and your coins may be locked for a while.
Tokenized asset
A token that represents a real-world asset, such as a fund, a bond or a stock. PriceCheck NG labels these and keeps them out of the main ranking.
Total supply
The number of coins that exist now, minus any that were destroyed. It can include coins that are locked and not yet in circulation.
Trading volume (24h)
The total value of a coin that was traded in the last 24 hours. Higher volume usually means it is easier to buy and sell. See how we count it.
Volatility
How much and how fast a price moves. Crypto is very volatile, which means a price can rise or fall a lot in a short time.
Whale
A person or organisation that holds a very large amount of a coin. When a whale buys or sells, the price can move.
