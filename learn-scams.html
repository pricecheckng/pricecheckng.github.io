How to avoid crypto scams in Nigeria

A free beginner guide. Learn the common tricks, the red flags and the safety steps, then test yourself with the quiz.

Golden rule: nobody can guarantee profit. Anyone who promises "daily returns", "double your money" or "zero risk" is lying or running a scam.

1. The scams you will meet most

💸
"Double your money" and daily-profit platformsThey pay the first people with money from new members, then disappear. Early "proof of payment" is how they recruit you.
🧾
Fake payment in P2P tradesThe buyer sends a fake bank alert or screenshot, or a transfer that later reverses, and asks you to release the crypto fast.
🎭
Fake support and impersonationSomeone on Telegram, WhatsApp or Instagram says they are "support" or an admin and offers to fix your account. Real support never messages you first and never asks for your password, code or recovery phrase.
🎁
Fake giveaways and airdrops"Send 0.1 and get 0.2 back" or "connect your wallet to claim". The link steals your funds.
📣
Signal groups and pump groupsPaid groups promise "sure profit signals". The organisers sell to you at the top. Nobody can predict the market for sure.
🔗
Phishing links and fake appsA look-alike website or app that copies a real exchange or wallet and steals your login.

3. Selling in P2P: stay safe

  1. Stay inside the app. Use the exchange's chat and escrow. Never agree to "trade outside" or "move to WhatsApp".
  2. Only release when the money is in your own bank app. A screenshot, an SMS or an email is not proof. Open your real bank app and check the balance.
  3. Be careful with transfers that say "pending". Wait until it is fully credited. Some fake transfers reverse later.
  4. The name must match. The payer's name must match the name on the exchange account. Do not accept payment from a third party.
  5. Check the trader. Look at completed trades and completion rate. Be careful with brand-new accounts and very good-looking prices.
  6. Never rush. Scammers use pressure: "I am in a hurry, release now". A real buyer can wait a few minutes.

4. Protect your own accounts

  • Never share your password, 2FA code or recovery phrase (seed phrase) with anyone. Not with "support", not with a friend.
  • Turn on two-factor login with an authenticator app, not only SMS.
  • Open exchanges from the official app or by typing the address yourself, not from links in messages.
  • Use a different password for every account.
  • If a deal looks too good, wait a day and ask someone you trust.

5. Red flag checklist

  • Promises of guaranteed or daily profit
  • Pressure to act now
  • Someone you don't know sends you a message first
  • They ask for your code, password or recovery phrase
  • They ask you to pay a "fee" first to receive money
  • They ask you to recruit friends to earn more
  • They want you to move the chat to another app

6. If it already happened

  1. Stop sending money. Scammers often ask for "one more fee" to release your funds.
  2. Keep evidence: screenshots, account numbers, usernames and links.
  3. Call your bank right away and report the transfer. The sooner you report, the better the chance of stopping it.
  4. Tell the exchange or platform you used.
  5. Report to the police and to the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC).
  6. Change your passwords and turn on two-factor login.
