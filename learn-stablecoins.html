What is a stablecoin?

A free beginner lesson on USDT, USDC and why they matter in Nigeria.

1. The idea

A stablecoin is a crypto coin designed to stay close to a normal currency, usually 1 coin = 1 US dollar. USDT (Tether) and USDC are the best known. When Bitcoin moves up and down, a stablecoin is meant to stay near $1.

2. Why people use them

  • Sending value: you can send dollars across the world in minutes.
  • A calm place to wait: traders move into a stablecoin when they don't want price swings.
  • Trading pairs: most coins are priced against USDT on exchanges.
  • Nigeria: many people use USDT in P2P trades to buy and sell dollars against naira.

4. How a stablecoin stays at $1

The company behind it says it keeps reserves (cash and similar assets) to back every coin. If you hold 100 USDT, you should be able to exchange it for about 100 dollars. How open each company is about its reserves differs, so the trust you place in the issuer matters.

5. Risks to know

  • Losing the peg: a stablecoin can drop below $1 if people lose trust in it.
  • Issuer risk: a company can freeze coins or have problems with its reserves.
  • Wrong network: USDT exists on several networks (for example TRC20, ERC20, BEP20). If you send to an address on a different network than the one it expects, the money can be lost. Always check that the network matches on both sides, and send a small test first.
  • P2P scams: read our guide on how to avoid crypto scams before your first trade.
  • Not interest-free from the naira: a stablecoin protects you from crypto swings, but the naira rate to dollars can still change.

6. USDT or USDC?

Both aim to stay at $1. They are issued by different companies. Many people use USDT because it is available almost everywhere in P2P, while USDC is also widely used. You can see both on PriceCheck NG.
